Six ways BitSov could look

Test #5 found the app honest but heavy: a long onboarding page, technical copy, no motion, and orange used for everything. Each direction below mocks the same three screens with the same sample data: picking a node, a chat with a priced send, and the wallet. Every mock keeps the doctrine: prices are ceilings, unknowns stay unknown, and custody is labelled.

Screenshots:

Recommendation: Ember 2.0, with three borrowed rules

  • Base on F. Keep the warm identity and the serif wordmark, rebuilt on accessible colour scales, with a light theme that gets equal care.
  • From A: orange means money. Buttons and outgoing bubbles go neutral. Orange is kept for prices, the send action and the wallet.
  • From C: the fee breakdown. The price chip opens “Maya’s price 2 · routing at most 5 · you pay at most 7”. A settled message shows what it actually cost.
  • From D: purposeful motion. A message slides in, the price highlights while sending, then settles. Reduced motion turns this into a fade.
  • Onboarding in three focused steps (node, identity, optional sats) replaces today’s single long page.

No new JavaScript dependencies. The only addition is about 180 KB of local OFL font files bundled with the app.

What every mock keeps

Prices are ceilings“up to 7 sats” before sending; actual cost after, such as 3 sats (2 + 1 routing).
Unknown stays unknownSpendable and reserved amounts say “Not reported”. No fiat value without a rate.
Custody, honestly“Self-custody, this Mac” or “your server”. No hosted option, because none exists.
No connection claims“Last paid message 10:21”, never “online” or “connected”.
